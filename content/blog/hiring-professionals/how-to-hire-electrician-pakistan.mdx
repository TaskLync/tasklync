Electrical problems are the one home repair category where the stakes of hiring the wrong person are highest. Bad plumbing is expensive. Bad electrical work kills people.

Pakistan's residential electrical infrastructure, with its voltage fluctuations, overloaded circuits, ageing wiring in older properties, and relentless load shedding cycles, makes professional competence even more important than in more stable grids. This guide gives you what you need to hire safely.

---

## Know What You're Hiring For

Electrical work covers a wide range of skill levels. Matching the job to the right professional matters.

| Job Type | Who You Need |
|---|---|
| Replace a switch or socket | General electrician (bijli wala) |
| Install a ceiling fan or light fixture | General electrician |
| Install AC power point | Experienced electrician |
| Distribution board upgrade or expansion | Licensed electrical contractor |
| New wiring for a room or floor | Licensed electrical contractor |
| UPS/inverter system installation | Electrician with electronics experience |
| Solar panel system installation | Qualified solar installer |
| ELCB/RCCB installation | Licensed electrician |

For anything involving the main distribution board (DB), new circuits, or the incoming supply, hire a licensed electrical contractor — not a general electrician who does basic switch replacements.

## Vetting an Electrician: Questions That Matter

Unlike plumbing where a bad joint is usually visible quickly, electrical faults can be invisible for months. Vetting matters more here.

**Ask about licensing.** Electricians who work on distribution boards and new circuits should hold a wireman's or electrical contractor's license from PEPCO or the relevant provincial authority. Ask to see it. A legitimate professional has it.

**Ask a technical question specific to your job.** For example: "My house doesn't have an ELCB — how would you install one and what size would you recommend?" or "The circuit for my AC keeps tripping — what are the three most likely causes?" A qualified electrician answers these easily. An unqualified one guesses or deflects.

**Ask about earthing.** Pakistan's residential earthing quality is frequently poor, and a good electrician cares about it. Ask: "How would you check and improve the earthing on my DB?" If they seem uninterested in earthing, that tells you something important about their work standard.

**Check their tools.** A professional electrician arrives with a multimeter, insulation tester, and proper hand tools. Someone who shows up with only a screwdriver and a basic tester is not equipped for serious work.

## What to Agree Before Work Starts

**Scope in writing.** "Fix the wiring" is not a scope. "Inspect and replace the faulty circuit breaker on the AC line in the first-floor DB, and test the earthing continuity on that circuit" is a scope.

**Materials quality.** Specify that copper wire of appropriate gauge be used (not aluminium, which is cheaper but far less safe for residential use). Ask what brand of circuit breakers they'll use — Siemens, Hager, and Schneider are recognised quality standards in Pakistan.

**Testing before payment.** Agree that you will test everything — all switches, sockets, the ELCB trip function — before final payment is made.

**No hidden helpers.** Agree on total price for the job including any assistant the electrician brings.

## During and After the Work

Be home when the electrician is working. For any DB work, be present and watch. Ensure the main supply is isolated before any work on live circuits begins. A professional will do this automatically — but confirm it.

After completion, test every socket and switch. Use a socket tester (available at any bijli shop for PKR 300–500) to verify correct wiring polarity on sockets. Test the ELCB trip function by pressing the test button. If anything fails to work correctly, it is resolved before payment — this is standard and any competent electrician will agree to it.

## Red Flags That Indicate Serious Risk

**Works on live circuits without isolating supply.** This is the most obvious competence and safety signal.

**Uses aluminium wire for new circuit installation.** Aluminium is cheaper but not appropriate for residential branch circuits.

**Dismisses earthing as unnecessary.** Earthing is not optional. Anyone who says so is not qualified.

**Refuses to test after completion.** No legitimate electrician objects to testing their own work.

**No multimeter or insulation tester.** These are basic tools. Their absence tells you about the quality of work you'll receive.

## The Bottom Line

Hiring an electrician in Pakistan requires more caution than hiring for almost any other home service — because the consequences of getting it wrong are severe and sometimes invisible until they're dangerous.

Take the time to vet properly. Ask the technical questions. Insist on quality materials. Test before you pay. These steps take an extra 30 minutes and can prevent a disaster.
