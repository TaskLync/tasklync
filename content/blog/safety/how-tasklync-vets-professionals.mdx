Hiring someone to work inside your home requires a level of trust that most people don't think through carefully until something goes wrong. You're letting a stranger into the place where you live, often when no one else is around, to handle things that affect your safety, your property, and your daily life. The question of whether that person is who they say they are, whether they're qualified to do what they've offered to do, and whether they'll behave responsibly when you're not watching, matters far more than most platforms have ever treated it.

The standard approach in home services is to build a marketplace, get as many professionals signed up as possible, and let reviews do the filtering. The problem with this model is that reviews only exist after something has already happened. A professional with no history on your platform has no reviews, no ratings, no record of anything, and yet they can still show up at your door. You are the person doing the first round of quality control, in your own home, with your own property on the line.

TaskLync was built on a different premise. Vetting happens before the first job, not after. Every professional on the platform has passed through a six-stage verification process that covers identity, background, licensing, insurance, skills, and ongoing conduct. None of the stages are optional. There is no fast track for professionals who seem trustworthy or who have good references. All six stages are completed before anyone is approved. This is what that process looks like and why each piece of it matters.

## Why Most Platforms Get This Wrong

It is worth being direct about the problem before getting into the solution, because the problem is more common than homeowners realize.

Most platforms in this space are designed to optimize for supply. The more professionals they have, the more jobs they can match, the more revenue they generate. Rigorous vetting slows supply down. It rejects applicants who would otherwise take jobs. It creates friction that not every platform is willing to accept. So the vetting gets light. A background check gets run, usually a basic one, and that becomes the main thing the platform can point to when a homeowner asks whether professionals are verified.

A background check alone is not vetting. It tells you whether someone has a recorded criminal history. It tells you nothing about whether their trade license is current, whether their insurance actually covers the work they're doing in your home, whether the references they listed are real people who actually employed them, or whether the certifications on their profile were earned or fabricated. These are not hypothetical gaps. They are the gaps that produce the bad outcomes homeowners actually experience.

The other failure mode is that platforms do real vetting at signup and then stop. A professional passes all the checks, gets approved, and from that point forward nothing is reviewed unless someone complains. Their insurance can lapse. Their license can be suspended. Their conduct on jobs can decline. None of it surfaces unless a homeowner files a dispute, and many homeowners don't, either because they don't know how or because they've already moved on.

The combination of shallow upfront screening and no ongoing monitoring is how platforms end up with professionals who were once legitimate and are no longer, or who were never as qualified as their profile suggested.

## The Six Stages, Explained

Every professional who applies to TaskLync goes through the same six stages in the same order. Passing one does not guarantee passing the next. Each stage is evaluated independently, and a failure at any point ends the application.

### Stage One: Identity Verification

The first thing we confirm is that the person applying is who they say they are. This sounds basic, but it is the foundation everything else rests on. A background check tied to a false identity tells you nothing about the real person standing in front of you. A license verified against a name that isn't real is worthless.

Every applicant submits a government-issued photo ID, a passport, driver's license, or national identity card, and completes a facial match verification against that document. The match is reviewed both by automated systems and by a human reviewer before it is cleared. We are confirming that the person who submitted the application is the same person whose identity document we're looking at. This step cannot be skipped, and it cannot be substituted with anything else.

### Stage Two: Background Check

Once identity is confirmed, we run a criminal background screening across relevant public databases. The check looks at criminal records at the national and local level, covering convictions and serious pending charges in categories relevant to the trust and safety of homeowners.

We do not run a single-database check and call it done. We check multiple sources, because records that appear in one database do not always appear in another, and gaps in single-source checks are well documented. The review is also not fully automated. A member of our trust and safety team looks at any result that requires context before a decision is made.

We are not trying to screen out everyone who has ever made a mistake. We are trying to identify patterns of behavior that represent a real risk to homeowners. Those two things are not the same, and we apply judgment accordingly.

### Stage Three: License Verification

This stage is the one most platforms either skip or handle poorly. For any trade that requires a license, we verify directly with the relevant authority that the license is current, that it covers the scope of work the professional intends to offer, and that it is in good standing with no active suspensions or disciplinary actions.

We do not accept copies of licenses as self-reported documents. We go to the source. For electricians, we verify with the licensing board in their jurisdiction. For plumbers, the same. For general contractors operating above the legal threshold for unlicensed work, we confirm the contractor's license is active and valid. If the license cannot be confirmed through the issuing authority, the professional is not approved for that trade category on the platform.

This matters because a license that cannot be verified is not a license. It is a document. The ability to do permitted work legally and with valid credentials is not optional for the people we approve.

<CalloutBlock variant="tip" title="Why license verification has to go through the issuing authority">
  Professionals can present expired licenses, licenses that belonged to someone else, or licenses that have been suspended. The only way to confirm that a license is real and current is to check with the authority that issued it. That is the only check we accept.
</CalloutBlock>

### Stage Four: Insurance Validation

General liability insurance protects the homeowner if a professional causes damage to their property while working. Workers' compensation protects the homeowner if a professional is injured on the job and the homeowner could otherwise be held liable. Both are required. Neither is optional.

We verify coverage and policy status directly, not by accepting a certificate the professional provides. Certificates can be fabricated. Policies can be cancelled after a certificate is issued. We confirm with the insurer that the policy is active, that the coverage amounts meet our minimums, and that the professional named on the policy is the one applying to the platform.

This verification is repeated as part of ongoing monitoring, which is covered in stage six. A professional who lets their insurance lapse after approval is not a professional we will continue to allow on the platform.

### Stage Five: Skills Assessment

The previous four stages confirm that a professional is who they say they are and that their credentials are legitimate. The skills assessment goes further and asks whether the quality of their work actually meets the standard homeowners on TaskLync should be able to expect.

This stage involves a review of work samples where applicable, reference checks with previous clients, and a review of any relevant certifications or completed training. The reference checks are real calls. We are not sending a form and accepting the written response. We speak with people who have hired this professional, ask specific questions about the quality of their work and their conduct on the job, and weight what we hear accordingly.

Not every professional passes this stage. A valid license and clean background do not automatically mean the work is good. Some applicants have legitimate credentials and produce substandard results. This is the stage that filters for quality, not just legitimacy.

### Stage Six: Ongoing Monitoring

Approval is not permanent. It is conditional on continued compliance with the standards that got a professional approved in the first place.

After a professional joins the platform, we monitor their ratings and reviews for patterns that suggest a decline in quality or a problem with conduct. We track disputes and investigate the ones that suggest something more serious than a one-off misunderstanding. We re-verify insurance status on a regular basis. And we review any information that surfaces through homeowner reports, platform activity, or external sources that might affect whether a professional should continue to work through TaskLync.

A professional who was excellent at the time of approval and becomes a problem six months later is still a problem. Ongoing monitoring is the mechanism that catches this and acts on it before more homeowners are affected.

## What Happens When Something Goes Wrong

We include this section because leaving it out would be incomplete. Vetting reduces the probability of bad outcomes. It does not reduce it to zero.

When a homeowner has a problem with a professional on TaskLync, the dispute process is handled by our trust and safety team directly. We review the job record, speak with both parties, and make a determination based on the evidence available. Depending on what we find, outcomes range from mediated resolution to formal warning, suspension, or permanent removal from the platform.

Professionals who are removed from the platform for conduct issues are not eligible to reapply. The removal is permanent, and the record is maintained.

We also act on credible reports that don't come through formal disputes. If a homeowner contacts us with concerns that don't rise to the level of a formal complaint but suggest a pattern worth monitoring, we take note of it and factor it into our ongoing review of that professional's standing.

The standard we hold ourselves to is simple: if we would not feel comfortable sending this professional to our own home, they should not be on the platform. That standard applies at the point of approval and continues to apply every day after.
