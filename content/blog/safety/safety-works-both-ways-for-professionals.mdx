Getting verified on TaskLync is the first test, not the only one. Passing identity checks, background screening, licence verification, and insurance validation earns a professional the badge. Keeping that badge depends on something separate, how they actually conduct themselves on every job after that. Here are the four standards every professional on TaskLync is held to, and why none of them are negotiable.

## Your Identity Is Your Access

The badge on a professional's profile is tied to one verified person, not a business, not a team, not whoever happens to be available that day. Sending someone else to a job under your name defeats the entire purpose of verification. The homeowner did not check that person's background, did not confirm their licence, and did not agree to let them into their home. They agreed to you.

This is treated as seriously as it sounds. Sending a substitute to a job in your name is grounds for immediate, permanent removal from the platform. There is no warning stage for this one, because the trust it breaks cannot be partially restored.

## Keep Payments in the Platform

Every payment on TaskLync moves through the platform and releases only after the job is confirmed complete. That structure exists to protect both sides, the homeowner has recourse if something goes wrong, and the professional has a guaranteed, trackable path to getting paid.

Asking a homeowner to pay in cash or transfer money outside the app breaks that protection entirely, and it is treated as a direct violation of conduct policy. It does not matter how the request is framed or how small the amount is. If money for a TaskLync job moves outside TaskLync, both sides lose the protection the platform is built to provide.

## Agree the Scope Before You Start

Home service jobs rarely stay perfectly within their original description, a small repair reveals a bigger issue, a job runs longer than expected. That is normal. What is not acceptable is deciding on your own that the scope has changed and charging for it after the fact.

Any additional work has to be quoted and accepted inside the app before it happens, not explained afterward on the invoice. Charges that were never approved through this process get reversed automatically. This protects homeowners from surprise costs, and it protects professionals too, because a scope that was agreed to in writing is a scope that cannot be disputed later.

## Your Rating Is Your Licence

Verification gets a professional onto TaskLync. Their rating is what keeps them there. Every completed job builds a visible track record, and that record is monitored continuously, not just glanced at occasionally.

Accounts that fall below 4.3 stars, or that carry a dispute rate above 2 percent, are reviewed by our trust and safety team and may be suspended depending on what that review finds. This is not about punishing a single bad day. It is about making sure a professional's rating on TaskLync actually reflects the experience homeowners can expect, consistently, not just on their best jobs.

## Why These Four Rules Exist Together

Each of these standards protects a different part of the same relationship. Identity protects who is actually doing the work. Payments protect how the money moves. Scope protects what a homeowner is agreeing to pay for. Ratings protect the ongoing quality of the experience. Take any one of them away, and the trust the entire platform is built on starts to erode from that exact spot.

Safety on TaskLync was never meant to be something homeowners get and professionals simply accept. It runs in both directions, and these four rules are what make that actually true in practice, not just in theory.

## The Bottom Line

Show up as the verified person a homeowner agreed to. Keep every payment inside the platform where it is protected. Get extra work approved before doing it, not after. Maintain a rating that reflects real, consistent quality. These four standards are what every professional on TaskLync commits to the moment they put on the badge, and they are what keep that badge worth having.
