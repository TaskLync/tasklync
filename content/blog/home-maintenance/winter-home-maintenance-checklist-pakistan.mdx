Winter in Pakistan gets underestimated. Lahore drops to near-freezing in January. Islamabad regularly sees sub-zero nights. Even Karachi — not known for cold — gets cold enough to spike gas demand, reveal poor insulation, and cause condensation problems that leave walls damp through February.

If you haven't prepared your home before temperatures drop, you're in for expensive surprises. This checklist covers everything Pakistani homeowners should inspect, service, or repair heading into the cold months.

## Gas System: The First Priority

For most Pakistani homes, gas is the backbone of winter — for geysers, cooking, and room heaters. A system that has sat idle all summer needs attention before you rely on it.

| Task | DIY or Pro? | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Inspect all gas heaters for soot, odour, or discolouration | DIY | Yellow or orange flames indicate incomplete combustion — a health hazard |
| Service room heaters and geysers | Pro | Annual service costs PKR 800–2,000 per appliance |
| Check gas flexible hose connections | Pro | Cracked rubber hoses are a common cause of gas leaks |
| Test all gas valves — open and close fully | DIY | Stuck valves should be replaced before you need them in an emergency |
| Ensure rooms with heaters have ventilation | DIY | Carbon monoxide from unvented heaters kills families every winter in Pakistan |

<CalloutBlock variant="danger" title="Never run a gas heater in a sealed room">
  Carbon monoxide poisoning from unvented gas heaters causes dozens of deaths across Pakistan every winter. Always keep a window slightly open when running a room heater — even in cold weather. If you smell gas, leave immediately and call SNGPL or SSGCL.
</CalloutBlock>

## Plumbing: Protect Your Pipes

Lahore, Islamabad, Murree, and all of northern Pakistan regularly see temperatures cold enough to freeze exposed water pipes. Even in warmer cities, cold snaps can catch unprepared homes.

### Pipe Insulation

Any pipe running on an exterior wall, in an unheated space, or through a roof space should be wrapped with foam pipe lagging before temperatures drop below 5°C. Pay particular attention to the pipes feeding overhead tanks — these are the most commonly frozen pipes in Pakistani homes.

### Geyser and Water Heater

Run your geyser and check for delayed heating, unusual sounds, or discolouration in the hot water. Check the pressure relief valve for corrosion. Geysers that have built up sediment over summer heat poorly and recover slowly — a PKR 1,500–3,000 flush and service keeps them efficient through the cold months.

### Outdoor Taps

If you have exposed outdoor taps in areas that freeze, ensure they can be shut off from inside. A cracked outdoor tap from a freeze is a PKR 500 part but requires draining the line before it splits.

## Roof and Exterior Walls

### Fog and Frost Damage

Winter fog in Punjab — particularly dense from December through February — deposits moisture on every surface. This wicks into any existing crack in exterior plaster or roof joints. Inspect exterior walls for cracks and apply a weather-resistant exterior filler before winter sets in. Pay attention to wall-to-roof junctions and parapet copings.

### Roof Drainage

Post-monsoon, check that roof drains are still clear. Winter fog and occasional rain still require working drainage. A blocked drain that went unnoticed after September monsoon will pond water on the roof through winter.

| Exterior Task | DIY or Pro? |
|---|---|
| Seal exterior wall cracks | DIY (small), Pro (large) |
| Check and clear roof drains | DIY |
| Inspect window sealant and putty | DIY |
| Check boundary wall cap for cracks | DIY |

## Heating and Insulation

Pakistani homes are not typically built for heat retention. Bare concrete walls, single-pane windows, and gaps under doors mean indoor temperatures track outdoor temperatures far more than they should. A few low-cost measures make a significant difference.

**Practical winter insulation steps for Pakistani homes:**

- Install door draft excluders on all exterior doors (PKR 200–500 each — among the highest-return items on this list)
- Apply window insulation film on single-pane windows in rooms you use most
- Hang heavy curtains — they reduce heat loss through glass by 30–40%
- Seal gaps around window AC units that aren't in use (a common cold air entry point)

For homes with false ceilings, check that ceiling insulation (if any) is intact. Heat rises, and poorly insulated ceilings make heating any room much harder.

## Electrical: Load Shedding and Winter Safety

Winter load shedding in Pakistan is significant, and electric heaters place heavy demand on wiring that may not have been designed for it.

| Task | DIY or Pro? |
|---|---|
| Check wiring capacity if using electric heaters | Pro |
| Inspect plug sockets used for high-draw appliances | Pro |
| Test ELCB and MCBs | Pro |
| Service UPS batteries | DIY/Pro |
| Check for exposed wiring near water entry points (condensation, leaks) | Pro |

If you're running a 2kW or 3kW electric heater on wiring that was installed 15 years ago, have an electrician check the circuit. Overloaded circuits are a leading cause of house fires in Pakistan during winter.

## Quick-Reference Winter Checklist

**Gas System**
- Service geysers and room heaters
- Check flexible gas hoses
- Confirm ventilation in heated rooms
- Test all gas valves

**Plumbing**
- Insulate exposed pipes in cold zones
- Check and service geyser
- Confirm outdoor taps can be shut off from inside

**Exterior**
- Seal wall cracks before fog season
- Clear roof drains
- Check window sealant

**Heating and Insulation**
- Fit door draft excluders
- Seal window AC gaps
- Check ceiling insulation

**Electrical**
- Verify circuit capacity for heaters
- Test ELCB and breakers
- Service UPS batteries
